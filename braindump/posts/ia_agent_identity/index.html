The subject

what
the identity of an AI agent.
  • what: what the term names.
  • what: who defines it, and why.
  • what: what problems its definition is meant to answer.

How Otsuka, Toyoda and Leung define the term

what
they define AI identity as the continuous relationship between what an agent is declared to be and what it is observed to do.1
what
they bound that relationship by the confidence that the two correspond at any given moment.1
therefore
on their definition identity is time-indexed and bounded by a confidence, not a fixed attribute.

What Otsuka, Toyoda and Leung find missing

what
they evaluate current technical and regulatory documents against the identity requirements of autonomous agents.2
what
they find none adequately addresses governing nondeterministic, boundary-crossing entities.2
what
they identify five critical gaps, among them recursive delegation accountability and agent identity integrity.3

The path argued as the central object

what
Kaptein, Khan and Podstavnychy formalize a compliance policy as a deterministic function from agent identity, partial path, proposed next action and organizational state to a violation probability.4
what
they argue the execution path is the central object for effective runtime governance.5
what
on their account static access control ignores the path, and so accounts only for a subset of all possible paths.6
