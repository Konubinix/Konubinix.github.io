Ia Agent Identity

Fleeting

tl;dr
RFC 8693 lets a consumer consider only the top-level claims and the current actor; AgentCore’s two principal types are OAuth-authenticated users and IAM-authenticated callers, the agent being no principal type of its own; one preprint argues the execution path is the central object.
authorship
this note is edited using Claude/default, governed by the note how to write a literature note

The subject

what
the identity of an AI agent, in the context of writing a policy engine that restricts AI agents.

How Otsuka, Toyoda and Leung define the term

what
they define AI identity as the continuous relationship between what an agent is declared to be and what it is observed to do.1
what
they bound that relationship by the confidence that the two correspond at any given moment.1
therefore
on their definition identity is time-indexed and bounded by a confidence, not a fixed attribute.

What a token can say about an agent

what
RFC 8693 gives a JWT an act claim, expressing that delegation occurred and naming the party acting.2
how
a chain of delegation is written by nesting one act claim inside another, the outermost being the current actor.3
rule
for applying access control policy, a token consumer must consider only the top-level claims and the current actor.4
therefore
a rule bound to an earlier party in the chain lies outside what the specification lets a consumer act on.

What a shipping policy engine binds to

what
in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, a Cedar policy names a principal — the entity making the authorization request.5
what
it supports two principal types, one for OAuth-authenticated users and one for IAM-authenticated callers.6
therefore
the agent is not a principal type of its own.
what
an IamEntity principal carries the caller’s IAM ARN, which for an assumed role names the role and enables stable principal == matching.7
therefore
two callers under one assumed role satisfy the same equality test on that id.
how
a policy session groups related invocations, and a temporal policy conditions on what happened earlier in that session.8
scope
the source is AWS documenting its own product, and is undated living documentation.

What the MCP specification requires

what
the 2025-06-18 revision places authorization at the transport level, for clients acting on behalf of resource owners.9
what
that revision states that authorization is optional for MCP implementations.10
therefore
an implementation conforming to it may carry no authorization at all.
scope
whether a later revision still says so is unchecked here.

What Otsuka, Toyoda and Leung find missing

what
they evaluate current technical and regulatory documents against the identity requirements of autonomous agents.11
what
they find none adequately addresses governing nondeterministic, boundary-crossing entities.11
what
they identify five critical gaps, among them recursive delegation accountability and agent identity integrity.12

The path argued as the central object

what
Kaptein, Khan and Podstavnychy formalize a compliance policy as a deterministic function from agent identity, partial path, proposed next action and organizational state to a violation probability.13
what
they argue the execution path is the central object for effective runtime governance.14
what
on their account static access control ignores the path, and so accounts only for a subset of all possible paths.15
