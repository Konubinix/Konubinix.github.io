The goal is to fill all my 5L cans in a single pour: water enters at the top, fills the first can, overflows into the second, and so on down the line.

A first attempt — cascade overflow funnel — drowned in complexity. Two issues killed it:

  1. Threading is harder to print than I had hoped, and stacking a threaded piece on top of another forces me to design gasket seats and the like.
  2. The funnel itself is hard to print (I still don’t know why) and takes too much space.

The simpler design replaces the funnel with a slanted ramp pierced by a hole. Under the hole, a short downspout fits snugly inside the can’s neck. Water enters at the top of the ramp, runs down, and falls through the hole into the can. Once the can is full, the water overflows past the hole and continues down the ramp onto the next can’s ramp.

Two constraints shape the geometry:

  • The slant must be shallow enough to keep many cans fitting side by side — a steep ramp eats vertical space — but steep enough that water reaches the hole rather than spilling off the side.
  • The lower end of each ramp must be narrower than its higher end, so that the lower end of the upstream ramp rests naturally on the higher end of the downstream ramp.

This design uses CadQuery: its Python API builds parts the way one would draw them — sketch, extrude, fillet — with named intermediate features that survive growth.

The ramp body

The ramp is a slanted slab — a constant-thickness prism tilted by the slope. Constant thickness keeps the filament budget close to a flat slab’s, regardless of how long the ramp gets. We sweep the U-shaped cross-section along the ramp’s length axis-aligned, then tilt the result. The trough runs the slab’s full length, open at both ends: water arrives at the high end, runs down the bed, drops through to the can when there’s room, and continues past the low end onto the next ramp once the can is full.

The slab spans the gap between two adjacent cans, with margin. Its slope balances the two limits from the introduction. The drain sits midrun; with the trough open on both ends, the exact spot doesn’t matter much.

Walls under the trough and along its sides come from a shared budget — sized so each side matches the downspout’s wall, the funnel between them keeping a uniform thickness. The trough is deep enough to hold flowing water without spilling over.

In plan view, the slab is mostly rectangular — wide enough for the drain hole to take the full trough width — tapering at the low end into a tongue that nests onto the next ramp’s high end (the second constraint from the introduction). The tongue ends in a short parallel section, so it engages the receiving trough along a length rather than at a single cross-section.

We have two important constraints:

  1. the funnel wall must be big enough to print easily, like 6mm or so
  2. the funnel must start falling at the end of the ramp wall -> the width of the ramp wall must be the same as the width of the funnel wall

import math

RAMP_LENGTH = 175
RAMP_SLOPE = 0.10
FUNNEL_WALL_WIDTH = 6
RAMP_WALL = FUNNEL_WALL_WIDTH
RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH = 2
HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW = RAMP_LENGTH / 2
RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH = 60
RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW = 44
RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH = 12
TAPER_LENGTH = 40
TONGUE_TIP_LENGTH = 15

slab_thickness = RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH + RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH
slope_rad = math.atan(RAMP_SLOPE)
slope_deg = math.degrees(slope_rad)

The cross-section is U-shaped — outer walls flanking the trough, floor below — and we sweep it along the ramp’s length: a constant narrow tip, a taper widening above it, then a constant wide section running to the high end. CadQuery’s multisection sweep splines smoothly between sections, bowing the sides; a ruled loft instead joins consecutive sections with straight surfaces, keeping the constant sections constant and the taper between them planar.

def u_section(width):
    outer = width / 2
    inner = (width - RAMP_WALL) / 2
    return [
        (-outer, 0),
        (-outer, slab_thickness),
        (-inner, slab_thickness),
        (-inner, RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH),
        ( inner, RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH),
        ( inner, slab_thickness),
        ( outer, slab_thickness),
        ( outer, 0),
    ]

slab = (
    cq.Workplane("YZ")
    .polyline(u_section(RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW)).close()
    .workplane(offset=TONGUE_TIP_LENGTH)
    .polyline(u_section(RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW)).close()
    .workplane(offset=TAPER_LENGTH)
    .polyline(u_section(RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH)).close()
    .workplane(offset=RAMP_LENGTH - TAPER_LENGTH - TONGUE_TIP_LENGTH)
    .polyline(u_section(RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH)).close()
    .loft(ruled=True)
)

The trough floor is the bed where the funnel will descend from, so we tag it. Under the hood, CadQuery’s tags don’t survive rotation, so the bed’s plane is pre-computed from the slope and re-attached after the slab is tilted around its low end.

bed_plane = cq.Plane(
    origin=(-RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH * math.sin(slope_rad), 0, RAMP_FLOOR_DEPTH * math.cos(slope_rad)),
    xDir=(math.cos(slope_rad), 0, math.sin(slope_rad)),
    normal=(-math.sin(slope_rad), 0, math.cos(slope_rad)),
)
slab = slab.rotate((0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), -slope_deg)
slab = cq.Workplane(bed_plane).add(slab.val()).tag("bed")

Adding a funnel and a downspout

Two cans, two neck diameters — the Netto/Ardea narrower, the MIEUXA wider. Rather than parameterising the geometry on the can, we print only the Netto version and use an adapter to fit the wider MIEUXA neck. The Netto value drives the ramp; both feed the adapter.

NECK_INNER_D = 35


NECK_INNER_D = 34.3

Below the hole, a funnel narrows into a downspout that seats in the can’s neck.

That seat has to hold despite printer tolerances and can-to-can variation. A uniform-diameter cylinder fails — too snug it won’t go in, too loose it rattles. A slight cone, looser at the bottom, slides in and wedges itself as it seats.

Where the funnel slants, the shell becomes nearly overhanging at its corners. The wall there must span the per-layer bead offset, or pinholes leak between layers.

DOWNSPOUT_DROP = 12
DOWNSPOUT_WALL = 3.2
DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS = NECK_INNER_D / 2
DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE = 0.4
FUNNEL_DEPTH = 10

The funnel’s top spans the full trough width — water flowing down the bed can’t bypass the hole until the can fills and the level rises back into the trough. Its bottom is world-horizontal so the cylinder hangs straight despite the slab’s slope.

outer_hole_w = RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH
inner_hole_w = outer_hole_w - FUNNEL_WALL_WIDTH

bed_p = slab.workplaneFromTagged("bed").plane
hole_center = bed_p.toWorldCoords((HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW, 0, 0))

outer_loft = (
    cq.Workplane().add(
        slab.workplaneFromTagged("bed")
        .center(HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW, 0)
        .rect(outer_hole_w, outer_hole_w)
        .val()
    ).toPending()
    .workplane(offset=-FUNNEL_DEPTH)
    .center(hole_center.x, 0)
    .circle(DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS + DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE)
    .workplane(offset=-DOWNSPOUT_DROP)
    .circle(DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS - DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE)
    .loft(ruled=True)
)

The funnel wall must be flush with the trough’s side walls — a ledge would pool water and let it bypass the hole. The same wall thickness carries through into the downspout, keeping the cross-section constant throughout.

inner_loft = (
    cq.Workplane().add(
        slab.workplaneFromTagged("bed")
        .center(HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW, 0)
        .rect(inner_hole_w, inner_hole_w)
        .val()
    ).toPending()
    .workplane(offset=-FUNNEL_DEPTH)
    .center(hole_center.x, 0)
    .circle(DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS - DOWNSPOUT_WALL + DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE)
    .workplane(offset=-DOWNSPOUT_DROP)
    .circle(DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS - DOWNSPOUT_WALL - DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE)
    .loft(ruled=True)
)

ramp = slab.union(outer_loft).cut(inner_loft)

Printing in two pieces

Hanging below the tilted slab, the funnel and downspout would print only with supports — and supports leave a rough surface exactly where the downspout seats in the can’s neck. Slicing the assembled ramp along its underside lifts the funnel out: the slab prints flat trough-up, the funnel flipped downspout-up, and glue bonds them back.

cutter = cq.Workplane("XY").transformed(rotate=(0, -slope_deg, 0)).rect(1000, 1000).extrude(1000)
ramp_top = ramp.intersect(cutter)
funnel_piece = ramp.cut(cutter)

Adding an extension to avoid spilling water

The tongue’s parallel tip reaches into the receiving ramp’s trough, but not far enough to bridge the gap reliably. An extension encloses the tongue’s U-shape and lengthens it — the tongue slides in through one open end. The side walls rise past the tongue and join overhead through a thin bridge. From that bridge, two lips cantilever down and cover the tongue’s wall top. Pushing the tip in flexes the cantilevers outward; once it clears, the lips spring back and trap it — no fastener needed. The extension continues past the tongue as an extension of the trough.

The lip’s inner edge aligns with the tongue’s trough wall, so its underside covers the wall’s full thickness.

EXTENSION_WALL = 3
EXTENSION_FLOOR = 2
EXTENSION_LIP_HEIGHT = 1
EXTENSION_BRIDGE_HEIGHT = 1
EXTENSION_CLEARANCE = 0.2
EXTENSION_LENGTH = 50

The cross-section is constant along the ramp axis — we sketch it once in the y-z plane and extrude for the tip’s length.

outer_w    = RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW + 2 * EXTENSION_CLEARANCE + 2 * EXTENSION_WALL
lip_bottom = slab_thickness + EXTENSION_CLEARANCE
lip_top    = lip_bottom + EXTENSION_LIP_HEIGHT
bridge_top = lip_top + EXTENSION_BRIDGE_HEIGHT
lip_inner  = (RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW - RAMP_WALL) / 2 + EXTENSION_CLEARANCE
lip_outer  = RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW / 2 + EXTENSION_CLEARANCE

extension_section = [
    (-outer_w/2,  -EXTENSION_FLOOR),
    (-outer_w/2,   bridge_top),
    (-lip_inner,   bridge_top),
    (-lip_inner,   lip_bottom),
    (-lip_outer,   lip_bottom),
    (-lip_outer,   0),
    ( lip_outer,   0),
    ( lip_outer,   lip_bottom),
    ( lip_inner,   lip_bottom),
    ( lip_inner,   bridge_top),
    ( outer_w/2,   bridge_top),
    ( outer_w/2,  -EXTENSION_FLOOR),
]

extension = (
    cq.Workplane("YZ")
    .polyline(extension_section).close()
    .extrude(EXTENSION_LENGTH)
)

Netto to Mieuxa adapter

The adapter bridges the two cans: it receives the Netto downspout at the top and plugs into the MIEUXA neck at the bottom. The transition between the two diameters is a conical frustum — no step on either surface, so the part prints without supports. The frustum wedges in twice: outside, the MIEUXA’s neck catches it at the right insertion depth; inside, anything dropped from above seats where the bore narrows.

Lengths are picked so each end engages enough of its mating neck.

NECK_INNER_D = 34.3
NETTO_NECK_INNER_D = NECK_INNER_D

NECK_INNER_D = 35
MIEUXA_NECK_INNER_D = NECK_INNER_D

DOWNSPOUT_DROP = 12
DOWNSPOUT_WALL = 3.2
DOWNSPOUT_INSERT_RADIUS = NECK_INNER_D / 2
DOWNSPOUT_CLEARANCE = 0.4
FUNNEL_DEPTH = 10

ADAPTER_FEMALE_LENGTH = 10
ADAPTER_JUNCTION_LENGTH = 3
ADAPTER_MALE_LENGTH = 10

The body uses sleeve_tapered() from the CadQuery pipe and sleeve shared block. Only the male end is tapered, to wedge snugly into the mieuxa neck regardless of the exact printed diameter; the female end stays cylindrical so the downspout slides all the way through (per-end tolerance=(female, male), female=0).

adapter = sleeve_tapered(
    cq.Workplane("XY"),
    female=(ADAPTER_FEMALE_LENGTH, NETTO_NECK_INNER_D, NETTO_NECK_INNER_D + DOWNSPOUT_WALL * 2),
    junction_length=ADAPTER_JUNCTION_LENGTH,
    male=(ADAPTER_MALE_LENGTH, MIEUXA_NECK_INNER_D - DOWNSPOUT_WALL * 2, MIEUXA_NECK_INNER_D),
    tolerance=(0, 1),
)

Netto to Netto adapter

Same as the Netto-to-Mieuxa adapter but with the male plug sized for a Netto neck. The canisters are the same height so no extra length is needed.
