Cascade Overflow Ramp

Fleeting

The goal is to fill all my 5L cans in a single pour: water enters at the top, fills the first can, overflows into the second, and so on down the line.

A first attempt — cascade overflow funnel — drowned in complexity. Two issues killed it:

  1. Threading is harder to print than I had hoped, and stacking a threaded piece on top of another forces me to design gasket seats and the like.
  2. The funnel itself is hard to print (I still don’t know why) and takes too much space.

The simpler design replaces the funnel with a slanted ramp pierced by a hole. Under the hole, a short downspout fits snugly inside the can’s neck. Water enters at the top of the ramp, runs down, and falls through the hole into the can. Once the can is full, the water overflows past the hole and continues down the ramp onto the next can’s ramp.

Two constraints shape the geometry:

  • The slant must be shallow enough to keep many cans fitting side by side — a steep ramp eats vertical space — but steep enough that water reaches the hole rather than spilling off the side.
  • The lower end of each ramp must be narrower than its higher end, so that the lower end of the upstream ramp rests naturally on the higher end of the downstream ramp.

The downspout

This design uses CadQuery rather than OpenSCAD. The previous attempt’s OpenSCAD code accreted into nested unions and differences that became hard to follow as the part grew; CadQuery’s Python API builds parts the way one would draw them — sketch, extrude, fillet — with named intermediate features that survive growth.

The downspout’s diameter is already pinned down: the previous attempt tuned a sealing skirt against the inner wall of each can’s neck, and we read the inner-neck Ø and wall thickness off those tests. For the Netto/Ardea (cap reference 40 mm, skirt 3.05 mm inward, wall 1.0 mm) that gives 33.9 mm. For the MIEUXA (cap reference 42 mm, skirt 3.6 mm inward, wall 1.2 mm), 34.8 mm. Sealing isn’t required for the ramp, but reusing the snug fit is a fine starting point.

NECK_INNER_D = 34.8
DOWNSPOUT_WALL = 1.2


NECK_INNER_D = 33.9
DOWNSPOUT_WALL = 1.0

The drop — the axial length of the downspout below the ramp’s underside — is the only new dimension. 12 mm gives a comfortable engagement into the neck for both cans.

DOWNSPOUT_DROP = 12

The body is a hollow cylinder, the outer radius landing on the inner-neck wall and the inner radius shrunk by the wall thickness.

downspout = (
    cq.Workplane("XY")
    .circle(NECK_INNER_D / 2)
    .circle(NECK_INNER_D / 2 - DOWNSPOUT_WALL)
    .extrude(DOWNSPOUT_DROP)
)

Standalone tangle per can, for an early print test before the ramp is designed: slip the downspout on the can’s neck and judge the fit by hand. If it binds or floats, tune DOWNSPOUT_WALL or NECK_INNER_D in the variant params.

Downspout MIEUXA (S 40/41)

import cadquery as cq

The ramp body

The ramp is a slanted slab — a constant-thickness prism tilted by the slope. Constant thickness keeps the filament budget close to a flat slab’s, regardless of how long the ramp gets. We build the slab axis-aligned (horizontal, trough cut from the top), tilt it to its slope, then drill a vertical hole through the tilted body — so the slab is slanted but the drain hole and downspout below stay vertical, dropping straight into the can’s neck. The trough is closed at the high end (so water arriving from above doesn’t spill backward) and open at the low end (so overflow continues onto the next ramp).

Run and slope come first. RAMP_LENGTH = 130 mm spans the gap between two cans with margin to spare. RAMP_SLOPE = 0.10 (about 6°) is mild enough to keep the stack short across many cans. HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW = 30 mm places the drain far enough from the open mouth that water is well into the trough before it falls through.

The trough is a rectangular channel: RAMP_TROUGH_WIDTH = 40 mm wide inside, RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH = 12 mm deep. RAMP_WALL = 2 mm sets every wall thickness — sides, bed, end-cap. The hole’s diameter follows the can: it equals NECK_INNER_D from the variant params.

The slab tapers in plan view: RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH = 50 mm at the high end shrinks to RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW = 44 mm at the low end, so the lower end of one ramp sits within the outline of the next ramp’s higher end — the second constraint from the introduction.

Slab thickness, the slope as an angle, and the hole’s world coordinates after tilting follow.

import math

RAMP_LENGTH = 130
RAMP_SLOPE = 0.10
HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW = 30
RAMP_TROUGH_WIDTH = 40
RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH = 12
RAMP_WALL = 2.0
RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH = 50
RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW = 44
HOLE_DIAMETER = NECK_INNER_D

slab_thickness = RAMP_WALL + RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH
slope_rad = math.atan(RAMP_SLOPE)
slope_deg = math.degrees(slope_rad)
hole_world_x = HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW * math.cos(slope_rad)
hole_world_z = HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW * math.sin(slope_rad)

The slab is a trapezoidal prism: the plan-view polygon extruded straight up by the slab thickness.

slab = (
    cq.Workplane("XY")
    .polyline([
        (0, -RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW / 2),
        (RAMP_LENGTH, -RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH / 2),
        (RAMP_LENGTH, RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_HIGH / 2),
        (0, RAMP_OUTER_WIDTH_LOW / 2),
    ])
    .close()
    .extrude(slab_thickness)
)

The trough cut is a rectangular box hollowing the slab’s top. It opens past the low end and stops one RAMP_WALL before the high end.

trough_cut = (
    cq.Workplane("XY")
    .box(
        RAMP_LENGTH - RAMP_WALL + 1,
        RAMP_TROUGH_WIDTH,
        RAMP_TROUGH_DEPTH + 1,
        centered=(False, True, False),
    )
    .translate((-1, 0, RAMP_WALL))
)
slab = slab.cut(trough_cut)

With the slab and trough in place, the body tilts around the low-end edge — the high end rises by slope_deg ≈ 6°, the low end stays anchored at the origin.

slab = slab.rotate((0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), -slope_deg)

The drain is drilled vertically through the now-tilted body at world X = hole_world_x — HOLE_OFFSET_FROM_LOW projected onto the horizontal. The cut is generously long in Z, so it passes through the slab regardless of where the tilt has carried the bed at this X.

hole = (
    cq.Workplane("XY")
    .center(hole_world_x, 0)
    .circle(HOLE_DIAMETER / 2)
    .extrude(slab_thickness + RAMP_LENGTH * RAMP_SLOPE + 2)
    .translate((0, 0, -1))
)
ramp_body = slab.cut(hole)

Putting it together

The downspout is placed below the drain at the slab’s tilted bottom face — world X = hole_world_x, world Z = hole_world_z — and drops straight down by DOWNSPOUT_DROP. The slab is slanted, but drain and downspout share a vertical axis, so the can sits below upright.

ramp = ramp_body.union(
    downspout.translate((hole_world_x, 0, hole_world_z - DOWNSPOUT_DROP))
)
