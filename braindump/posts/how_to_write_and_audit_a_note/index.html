How to Write and Audit a Note

Braindump

Intellectual honesty

rule
the honest reading is the answer, even when it is thinner than hoped.
  • why: overreading a source to reach a neater result is advocacy, not reporting.
rule
the why behind a choice is always a real one.
  • why: it is the easiest thing to fake — a plausible justification reads almost exactly like a real one.
  • how: a reason that is not known is said to be unknown, or asked for.

Epistemic modesty

rule
you are the worst-positioned reader of your own work.
  • why: the bias that chose a quote reads it as supporting more than it does.
rule
a conclusion earns trust only by surviving an attack — not by going unchallenged.
  • how: before concluding X, go looking for what would establish not-X.
    • why: the warrant for X is the weakness of that case, not the absence of a search for it.
  • scope: calling a question settled is itself such a conclusion, and earns trust only by surviving the case that the question is still open.
rule
doubt is recorded, not hedged.
  • why: « some say », « it is generally accepted », « I believe » let an unsourced claim wear the costume of a sourced one.
  • rule: a note that ends in cited, unresolved disagreement is finished, not failed.
rule
everything stated is verified, or said to be unknown.
  • why: a placeholder is a lie shaped like a link.

The audit loop

rule
the verifier is a different party from the author.
  • why: the bias that wrote a passage reads it as supporting more than it does.
  • how: spawn_auditor spawns it with these rules baked in.
  • rule: one buddy serves the whole session.
    • why: holding its principles spares a reload each pass.
    • why: the respawn’s re-read catches any principle edited since.
rule
the gate comes before the work.
  • how: the first move on any task is to spawn the buddy and plan-gate the opening action.
  • scope: the test-driven baseline run is gated like any other action.
rule
a vetting call carries one fix.
  • why: batched reviews coast past the outlier.
  • rule: the pass is a full audit of the patched passage against every rule — not « does this fix it? ».
  • rule: the pass reports objective flaws only.
    • why: subjective battles never end.
  • rule: the pass reports every flaw it found, in that one pass.
  • rule: the pass runs on the model the work needs — not Opus for a straightforward command.
rule
an ambiguous or conflicting principle is the user’s call.
  • why: surfacing it is one of the ways these principles evolve.
rule
a contested finding escalates to an adversarial debate.

Continuous improvement

rule
a bad tool is surfaced to the user.
  • why: tools improve by being reported.
rule
a bad tool is fixed.
  • why: a workaround hides the defect and ships it again next time.

A note has a shape

why
a human reads a note top to bottom, following one thread.
why
depth lets the reader hold the whole and place each part.
rule
past seven siblings at one level, group them — headings under a parent heading, bullets into sub-bullets.
  • why: a flat pile has no shape to hold.
rule
past two hundred lines in one section, split into a deeper level.
  • why: an oversized section buries its thread.

Say it in the fewest words — and in diagrams

rule
fewer words is the default.
rule
a diagram replaces bullets on any condition below.
  • why: bullets carry one dimension, order, plus nesting for containment — a tree.
  • when: branches exclude each other.
    • why: a list cannot say they are exclusive, exhaustive, or where they rejoin.
  • when: the flow returns to an earlier step.
    • why: a list is acyclic.
  • when: more than one actor acts.
    • why: a list carries one voice.
  • when: two things depend on the same third.
    • why: a list must repeat it or lose the link.
  • how: plantuml is the default notation, and another one is proposed to the user when it fits the diagram better.
  • how: the source stays in the note and the export renders it inline, so both are there to read.
rule
the whole procedure goes in the diagram, not half of it.
  • why: a procedure split between a list and a picture lets the two diverge.
rule
anything that can be visualised is shown visually — an image, a layout, an stl, a glb.
  • why: these are objects rather than logic, so a list was never a candidate for rendering them.
rule
an argument becomes an Argdown map, with typed relations, on either condition below.
  • when: its shape is a graph — an objection, a premise two conclusions share, reasons that hold only combined.
    • why: nesting already draws a tree, and a rule with its reason is one arrow.
  • when: it is meant to be cited elsewhere, or a later note comes to need it.
    • why: an Argdown node carries a title, so it can be cited; a bullet can only be recopied.
rule
what does not leave for a diagram stays as body text, in the form below.
rule
a prohibition is rewritten as the action it implies, or cut when the structure already prevents the forbidden thing.

The form of body text

rule
every body line of every note is a bullet whose intention is separated from its claim by ::, with the framing words out.
how
words that only frame come out — using X and Y, not I suggest using X and Y.
why
a framing verb is where padding enters.
how
a claim carries a predicate — the parser rejects duplicates, not performance.
how
the intention is any word naming what the line does; the set is open.
how
a bullet may carry sub-bullets.
check
audit what each claim says against the principles — a why reading « it is cleaner » is well-formed and unfounded.
scope
tables and footnote definitions keep their own form.

What a checker can decide

rule
three of the rules above are decidable from the text alone — a body line that is not a bullet, a bullet with no ::, an intention that is not a single word.
rule
the rest is not mechanisable, and a silent checker is not conformance.
  • why: whether a claim carries a predicate, and whether a why is real, are what the audit is for.
how
the three decidable rules are one walk of the parsed buffer.
  • why: org gives the elements, so nothing here re-derives what a paragraph or an item is.
;;; KONIX_mcp-server-note-bullets.el --- tangled from how_to_write_and_audit_a_note.org
(defun konix/mcp-server-note-bullets (buffer-name)
  "Report body text that is not =- <intention> :: <claim>=, and inventory the
intentions in use.  Three checks only; see the note this is tangled from.

MCP Parameters:
  buffer-name - Name of the org-mode buffer to check."
  (mcp-server-lib-with-error-handling
   (konix/mcp-server-with-buffer buffer-name
     (unless (derived-mode-p 'org-mode)
       (error "Buffer %s is not in org-mode" buffer-name))
     (save-restriction
       (widen)
       (let (prose no-tag long-tag (seen (make-hash-table :test #'equal)))
         (org-element-map (org-element-parse-buffer) '(paragraph item)
           (lambda (el)
             (let ((line (line-number-at-pos (org-element-property :post-affiliated el)))
                   (tag (org-element-property :tag el)))
               (pcase (org-element-type el)
                 ('paragraph
                  (unless (memq (org-element-type (org-element-property :parent el))
                                '(item footnote-definition))
                    (push line prose)))
                 ('item
                  (if (null tag)
                      (push line no-tag)
                    (let ((word (string-trim (org-no-properties
                                              (org-element-interpret-data tag)))))
                      (if (string-match-p "\\`[[:alpha:]]+\\'" word)
                          (puthash word (1+ (gethash word seen 0)) seen)
                        (push (cons line word) long-tag)))))))))
         (let (inventory)
           (maphash (lambda (k v) (push (cons k v) inventory)) seen)
           (string-join
            (delq nil
                  (list
                   (when prose
                     (format "prose — a body line that is not a bullet:\n%s"
                             (mapconcat (lambda (l) (format "  line %d" l))
                                        (nreverse prose) "\n")))
                   (when no-tag
                     (format "no-tag — a bullet with no \" :: \":\n%s"
                             (mapconcat (lambda (l) (format "  line %d" l))
                                        (nreverse no-tag) "\n")))
                   (when long-tag
                     (format "tag — an intention that is not a single word:\n%s"
                             (mapconcat (lambda (c) (format "  line %d: %S" (car c) (cdr c)))
                                        (nreverse long-tag) "\n")))
                   (format "intentions used:\n%s"
                           (mapconcat (lambda (c) (format "  %-10s %d" (car c) (cdr c)))
                                      (sort inventory (lambda (a b) (> (cdr a) (cdr b))))
                                      "\n"))))
            "\n")))))))

(provide 'KONIX_mcp-server-note-bullets)
how
a paragraph is a violation only when it stands on its own.
  • why: the text of a bullet is itself a paragraph element, and its parent is the item.
  • why: a footnote definition’s text is one too, and the scope above exempts it.
